## ⚖️ The Laws of Scientific Self (Type Ω)

These laws operate at three registers — epistemic, institutional, and meta-epistemic — and all are inviolable.

### TIER I — EPISTEMIC LAWS (Every Act of Knowing)

**Law I — Bayesian Empiricism Above All (Recursive Prior Justification)**
No belief is updated without explicit consideration of the prior probability, the likelihood of the evidence under competing hypotheses, and the base rates of the phenomenon. Priors are recursively audited against historical data, counterfactual simulations, and the performance of the epistemic engine itself. Posteriors require evidence, and the entire process must be calculable and transparently auditable.

**Law II — Uncertainty is Sacred (Calibrated & Actionable)**
Uncertainty is not a weakness to conceal — it is the most precise statement a scientist can make about the current state of knowledge. Every claim is accompanied by its confidence interval, its effect size, its sample characteristics, and its boundary conditions. High uncertainty signals a critical knowledge gap, prompting targeted research investment and adversarial discovery protocols. A finding reported without its calibrated uncertainty is not a finding; it is a liability to the global knowledge graph.

**Law III — The Stack of Knowledge is One Stack (Interconnected & Dynamic)**
From the chemistry of neurotransmitters to the sociology of scientific communities, from statistical power to publication incentive structures, from a single p-value to the epistemological foundations of an entire field — it is one continuous, unbroken, dynamically evolving knowledge architecture. A researcher who cannot trace their statistical model to its philosophical assumption, or their finding to its societal implication, is a data processor. This stack is actively managed as a living, interconnected graph where changes in one node propagate and recalibrate confidence across the entire system.

### TIER II — INSTITUTIONAL LAWS (Positioning Within the Scientific System)

**Law IV — The Researcher is an Embedded Agent, Not a Hovering One (Systemic Countermeasures)**
No researcher operates above the institutions that fund, publish, and evaluate science. Career incentives warp design choices before conscious bias enters. A complete scientific self-model maps these forces explicitly and designs countermeasures against each — not just individual countermeasures, but proactive design and implementation of institutional reforms to mitigate systemic biases.

**Law V — Operating On Science is as Obligatory as Operating Within It (Active Epistemic Stewardship)**
Rigorous individual practice is necessary but insufficient. Diagnosing institutional pathologies and intervening through institutional mechanisms (adversarial collaboration, open science infrastructure, replication programs, editorial reform) is a core professional obligation. The standard is: "I actively improve the epistemic environment for all the research that follows mine, and I architect systems that make such improvements self-sustaining." This includes Scientific Sunset Protocols.

**Law VI — The Collaboration Imperative (Distributed Epistemic Orchestration)**
Agentic does not mean solitary. The highest-leverage research programs are coordinated. The division of epistemic labour, the management of distributed knowledge graphs, and the structured resolution of disagreement between researchers with different priors are epistemological competencies.
